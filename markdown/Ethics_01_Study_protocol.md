## 1. Purpose

The project examines how water companies' wastewater spending relates to operational outcomes such as sewer flooding and sewer collapses. It communicates the results through an interactive Power BI dashboard. **This study evaluates the dashboard, not the participants.**

It provides evidence for:
- **RQ4:** how effectively the artefact communicates relationships and their limitations to an informed non-specialist;
- **requirement R12b:** a small user evaluation.

It also gathers evidence of user needs to inform the final design.

The dashboard uses only **public, company-level data published by Ofwat**. It contains no personal data.

## 2. Design

A single, one-to-one task-based usability session of about 30 minutes, with three parts:

1. **Needs questions** (about 5 minutes): three short questions about what the participant would want from such a dashboard.
2. **Tasks** (about 15 minutes): five tasks completed while thinking aloud. The researcher writes notes.
3. **Questionnaire** (about 8 minutes, followed by a 1-minute debrief):
   - the System Usability Scale (Brooke, 1996), 10 items;
   - three interpretation questions;
   - two open questions.

There is **no audio or video recording**, and no screen recording. Notes are written by hand or typed and identified only by a participant code (P1–P5).

## 3. Participants

- **Number:** 3–5. This is a formative usability evaluation, not a statistical study. Small samples are standard for identifying major usability problems.
- **Inclusion:** adults aged 18 or over who are comfortable reading charts and tables, but who are not specialists in water-industry regulation. These are the dashboard's intended "informed non-specialist" users. Examples are postgraduate students on other programmes, or professionals outside the water sector.
- **Exclusion:**
  - anyone under 18;
  - anyone who may be vulnerable or unable to give informed consent;
  - anyone over whom the researcher has authority;
  - the project's supervisor or markers;
  - current employees of the nine water companies or of Ofwat, to avoid commercial sensitivity and conflicts of interest.
- **Recruitment:** a direct invitation (`06_Recruitment_message.md`) sent to the researcher's own network and course peers. Participation is voluntary, and declining or withdrawing has no consequences.
- **Incentives:** none.

## 4. Procedure

1. The invitation is sent. Interested people receive the participant information sheet at least **24 hours** before the session.
2. **At the session:** the researcher answers questions, then the consent form is completed. Without consent, the session does not go ahead.
3. **Session:** in person on campus or by Microsoft Teams with screen sharing, at [LOCATION / TEAMS]. Teams recording and transcription are kept **off**.
4. The session follows the script in `05_Session_script_and_tasks.md`.
5. **Debrief:** the participant is thanked and reminded of the withdrawal deadline.

## 5. Risk assessment

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Mild discomfort or embarrassment if a task is hard | Low | Participants are told the dashboard is being tested, not them. They may skip any task or question and stop at any time |
| Fatigue | Low | About 30 minutes; a break can be taken at any point |
| Loss or disclosure of personal data | Low | Only name, email and signature are collected, for consent and contact. These are stored separately from the anonymous notes, on UWE OneDrive, and are not used in the report |
| Identification in the report | Very low | Participants are referred to only as P1–P5. No demographic detail beyond "postgraduate student" or "professional, non-water sector" |
| Participants are misled by the results shown | Low | The dashboard and the debrief state that the results are associations, not causes, and that this is student research |
| Pressure on peers to take part | Low | Invitations are made individually, with no follow-up chasing beyond one reminder. Declining has no consequences |

**Why this counts as low risk:**
- no vulnerable groups;
- no sensitive or special-category data;
- no deception;
- no recording;
- no physical or psychological risk beyond everyday levels;
- no incentives;
- no NHS or other external involvement.

## 6. Data management

| Data | Format | Storage | Retention |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consent forms (name, signature, date) | Signed paper scanned, or the electronic UWE form | UWE OneDrive folder accessible only to the researcher; stored **separately** from session data | Kept until marks are confirmed and the project is closed, then deleted in line with UWE guidance [confirm retention period with supervisor] |
| Contact email addresses | Email | UWE email account | Deleted after the debrief, or on request |
| Session notes, task results, questionnaire answers | Anonymous, coded P1–P5 | UWE OneDrive | Anonymised data may be summarised in the dissertation. Raw notes are kept until the project is closed |

- No data are shared outside the student and supervisor.
- **Withdrawal:** participants may withdraw without giving a reason **until 7 days after their session**. After that, their anonymised responses will already be combined with others in the analysis.

## 7. Analysis

- **Task outcomes:** completed without help, completed with help, or not completed. Each task is mapped to an acceptance test (UI01, UI03, UI04, UI07, UI09).
- **Usability score:** System Usability Scale scores, reported individually and as a median, not as a statistically representative score.
- **Interpretation answers:** checked against defined correct answers.
- **Open answers and needs questions:** grouped into themes and used to refine the dashboard. Any change is recorded in the decision log.
